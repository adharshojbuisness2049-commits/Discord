Who finds a seat here

Δv

The trajectory people

You've run a Hohmann transfer by hand at least once. Porkchop plots make you happy in a way you've stopped trying to explain to people.

τ

The light-time pedants

You know there's a difference between "Mars is at opposition" and "the picture you're looking at is current." You will say so.

⟲

The satellite watchers

TLEs, pass predictions, the ISS overhead at dusk. You've stood in a backyard at the right minute more than once.

∞

The patient ones

Multi-year cruise phases don't bore you. You understand that the interesting part is often the part where nothing visibly happens.

What's actually discussed

#mission-tracking
Live threads on active missions — burns, flybys, comms windows, the moments where a spacecraft does something and you want to talk about it within the hour.
#orbital-mechanics
Worked problems, transfer windows, n-body weirdness, and the occasional argument about perturbation methods that runs four days too long.
#satellite-tracking
TLEs, pass predictions, ground station chatter, and people comparing notes on what they caught through a finderscope last night.
#instruments-and-gear
Telescopes, trackers, the homebrew rigs people build to log their own ephemerides. Hardware talk, not hype.
#the-archive
Long-form posts worth keeping — mission retrospectives, derivations, the kind of thing you'd want to find again in two years.
